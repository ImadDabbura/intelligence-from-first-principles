# Rethinking the Mind - Prof. Mark Solms

[Original episode](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=meUtWtY00H8) · [All reviews](../README.md) · [Learning path](../learning-path.md)

Published: 2024-09-18 · Decision: **Worthwhile** · Confidence: High for distinct conceptual relevance; moderate for the proposed consciousness theory and low for treating its strongest sufficiency claims as established.

## Central claim

Affect can be approached as a control process assigning organism-relative value to competing needs under uncertainty. This provides a useful counterweight to intelligence conceived solely as cortical perception and planning, but identifying adaptive regulation with phenomenal feeling remains a substantive additional claim.

## Ideas I want to keep

- Solms distinguishes the mechanisms supporting wakefulness from the contents and qualities of experience, then challenges the standard allocation of these roles to brainstem and cortex. He draws on developmental lesions, stimulation, imaging, and pharmacology rather than treating verbal report as the only evidence. The methodological lesson is triangulation across interventions and observations.

- Multiple viable-state constraints matter: sleepiness and thirst cannot be satisfied by reducing an undifferentiated total in whichever way is easiest. A controller needs state variables identifying which need is unmet and policies appropriate to that need. This is a concrete reason to study structured preferences rather than a single uninterpreted reward number.

- Flexible behavior becomes useful when a habitual regulatory response fails. The oxygen-room example distinguishes repeating a reflex from exploring actions and evaluating their consequences against internal needs. The transferable insight concerns uncertainty-triggered control and policy adaptation; the claimed necessity of feeling is not established merely by the example.

- The closing account links affect and attention through precision modulation: the relative confidence assigned to predictions, errors, and policies changes which information drives behavior. Exploration can increase immediate uncertainty while reducing future uncertainty, resolving a simplistic reading of homeostasis as seeking an unchanging environment.

- The discussion of REM sleep and dreaming illustrates the explanatory power of double dissociation: two frequently correlated phenomena need not be the same process. It also illustrates why clean inference requires distinguishing the measured phenomenon, reported experience, and the neural intervention.

## Mathematical anatomy

**Objects:** An organism with multiple internal variables and viable ranges, a generative model, candidate policies, sensory prediction errors, and precisions. Solms adds first-person valence and qualitative need categories to this control description.

**Objective:** Maintain conditions supporting continued existence while resolving uncertainty that obstructs action. A useful formal reconstruction is constrained control over a viability set; satisfying one need cannot compensate for violating another lethal constraint. This reconstruction does not claim the interview derives a particular controller.

**Mechanism:** Internal deviations recruit adaptive policy selection; predicted outcomes and incoming errors are weighted by confidence. Successful habits can execute with less deliberation, while failures recruit flexible search and learning.

**Assumptions:** The registered internal variables must track actual viability, the model must distinguish relevant needs, and precision estimates must be sufficiently calibrated. The step from these computational properties to subjective experience requires a bridging theory.

**Limits:** Variational free energy is model-relative evidence-bound machinery, not simply a numerical probability of death. A scalar objective can encode multiple constraints if its state representation preserves them, so thirst-versus-sleep does not refute scalar optimization. Statistical precision is not classification precision or recall.

## Evidence, limitations and corrections

- At 00:13:36–00:15:28 Solms explicitly accepts that intuiting another being is conscious is not sufficient evidence and argues for stimulation-based predictions. This is a more careful position than the introductory montage alone suggests.

- Necessity of an arousal structure does not establish that it alone is sufficient for all experience. Stimulation in a person with an intact cortex also acts within an interacting system. Developmental decortication and adult lesions are different interventions with different possibilities for reorganization.

- The argument at 00:47:14 that feelings are necessarily felt establishes a definition. It does not by itself show that a functional regulation mechanism produces phenomenal feeling. A competing controller could implement the same stated control operations without settling the consciousness question.

- The transcript has conspicuous name errors and a garbled double-dissociation sentence at 01:12:02. The preceding account clearly contrasts preserved dreaming after loss of REM with preserved REM after loss of dreaming. Primary clinical findings were not independently reanalyzed.

- Solms’s 2019 primary article confirms that precision optimization is central to his proposal, but it is an argument for a theory, not independent validation that the hard problem has been solved. Pharmacological anecdotes and broad summaries of competing theories should not be treated as decisive mechanistic tests.

## Relevance to my research

Worthwhile for my study of intelligence itself: endogenous needs, value and attention selection are architectural questions that prediction-only accounts can omit. I want to keep the control insights separate from the consciousness claim. This is a competing model to investigate rather than a replacement consensus to adopt.

## Objections and open questions

An engineering explanation of why a controller needs internal priorities does not establish why it feels. Conversely, failure to identify a necessary cortical region would not prove consciousness irrelevant to intelligence. The most productive response is to seek interventions that distinguish competing accounts, rather than replace one anatomical monocentrism with another.

## An experiment I could try

I would create a maze agent with energy and hydration constraints, then block its usual route to water. A fixed reflex controller, a scalar-reward learner retaining both internal state variables and a belief-state planner with uncertainty-triggered exploration would be compared, including misleading internal measurements. Survival and adaptation would be measured separately from the question of which observations could support a claim about phenomenal experience.
